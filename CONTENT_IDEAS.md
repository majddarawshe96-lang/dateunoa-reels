## Rules for anything we post
- One idea per video. Hook in the first 2 seconds, on screen as text.
- Never mock men or women as a group. Debate yes, contempt no.
- Always end with a reason to save/share/comment, and point to the link in bio.
- Captions: use the matching template in PLAYBOOK.md.

## Filmable scripts (15–35 s each)

### 1. "The one-question test" (talking head, sells Book II)
**On-screen hook:** If you're confused about him, do this.
> Mute everything he says. Only watch what he does.
> Does he make plans? Does he show up? Is his effort the same when you're not pulling away?
> If you only watched his actions, what would you conclude?
> …Yeah. That's your answer.
**End text:** Chapter 1 of "What Do Men Want?" · link in bio

### 2. "Cheap vs expensive" (talking head or faceless list, Book II)
**Hook:** He's paying you in the cheap currency.
> "I miss you" at 1 a.m.: cheap. Free to type.
> A plan with a day and a time: expensive.
> Compliments: cheap. Consistency: expensive.
> Stop getting excited about the cheap stuff.
**Caption hook:** Send this to the friend who's still decoding his "haha" 👀

### 3. "Men don't fall for perfect" (faceless b-roll, Book II)
**Text on screen, 3 beats:**
> Men don't fall for perfect.
> They fall for peace.
> No tests. No guessing games. Just a woman who says what she means, kindly.

### 4. "She's not hard to read" (talking head, a man's voice ideal, Book III)
**Hook:** Men, she's not hard to read. You're just not listening.
> She mentioned a restaurant twice. Book it.
> She told you about her interview. Ask how it went.
> She said "it's fine" with that voice. It's not fine. Ask again, softer.
> Paying attention is the whole cheat code.
**End text:** "What Do Women Want?" · link in bio

### 5. "Safe AND strong" (faceless, Book III)
> Strength without safety is scary.
> Safety without strength feels like friendship.
> Be both.

### 6. "The bird test" (POV skit or voiceover, Book III)
**Hook:** The bird test is real, and here's the science.
> Researchers watched newlyweds for years.
> The couples who stayed together turned toward each other's tiny bids for attention about 86% of the time.
> The couples who divorced? About 33%.
> So when she says "look at that bird"… look at the bird.
**Caption hook:** Tag someone who'd fail the bird test 🐦

### 7. "He says / He means" (green-screen over chat screenshots, Book II)
> He says: "I'm just bad at texting." He means: "You're not a priority yet."
> He says: "Let's not put labels on it." He means: "I want the benefits without the commitment."
> He says: "I'll book somewhere for Friday." He means: exactly that. Marry him. (joke)
**Caption hook:** Which one have you heard? Comment the number 👇

### 8. "She says / She means" (same format, Book III)
> She says: "I'm fine." She means: "Ask me one more time, gently."
> She says: "Whatever you want to do." She means: "Please just make a plan."
> She says: "I had fun!" and nothing else. She means: you need a better second date.

### 9. "Talking stage resume" (POV skit, Bundle)
**Hook:** POV: you've had enough talking stages.
> Show yourself typing a long message, deleting it.
> Then sending three PDF covers instead.
> Text: "Read these first, then we can talk."
**Caption:** All 3 books, $56. Buy 2, get 1 free 📚

### 10. "What I need is not much" (walking b-roll list, Book II)
> Consistent. Calm. Makes plans.
> Texts back. Says what he means.
> Not much to ask.

### 11. "Clarity is attractive" (talking head, Book III)
**Hook:** Men, the most attractive text you can send is boring.
> "Are you free Thursday? I'll book us somewhere."
> Not "wyd." Not three days of silence and then "hey stranger."
> Clarity is rare. That's why it works.

### 12. "Read receipts" (faceless, Book I)
> Read at 11:04.
> Online at 11:20, 11:41, 12:15.
> No reply.
> You don't need to text back. You need to believe what you just watched.
