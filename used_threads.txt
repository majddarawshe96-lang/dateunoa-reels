Things nobody tells you about situationships:
You can't lose someone you never really had. But it still hurts like you did.
The "what are we?" talk feels scary because part of you already knows the answer.
Being chosen "sometimes" slowly teaches you to be grateful for crumbs.
Mixed signals are a clear signal. They mean: "I'm not sure about you."
The right person won't make you audition for a title.
Walking away from something undefined isn't failing. It's choosing yourself out loud.
Texts that hurt more than they should:
"lol" in reply to something you were nervous to send.
"sorry, just seeing this" 9 hours later. After watching your story.
"we'll see" when you asked about the weekend.
"you're overthinking it" when you asked one simple question.
Nothing at all. After you finally said "I miss you."
None of these mean you're too much. They mean the reply was too little.
Men, small things she'll remember for years:
You texted "home safe?" without being asked.
You remembered the name of her difficult coworker.
You made the plan. Day. Time. Place. She didn't have to chase it.
You said "I'm not sure yet, but I like you" instead of disappearing.
You were the same man on a Tuesday as you were on Saturday night.
None of it costs money. All of it costs attention.
Signs you're in a situationship, not a relationship:
You know his coffee order but not if he's seeing anyone else.
Plans happen at 10pm. Never at 10am.
You've never met a single one of his friends.
You rehearse your texts so they don't sound like you want more.
You feel closest to him right before he pulls away.
You deserve something you don't have to decode.
What he says vs what he means (situationship edition):
He says: "I don't like labels." He means: "I like having you close without promising anything."
He says: "Let's not rush it." He means: "Let's not define it."
He says: "You're amazing, I just can't right now." He means: "I know what I'd lose. I'm still not choosing."
He says: "I've never felt like this." He means: maybe exactly that. Watch what he does next week.
Words open the door. Consistency is what walks through it.
She didn't "suddenly" lose interest. Here's what actually happened:
She asked to see you. You said "this week's crazy." Three weeks in a row.
She shared something real. You replied with a meme.
She stopped double-texting. You thought that meant everything was fine.
She got quieter. When she finally explained, you called it drama.
By the time she left, she'd been leaving for months.
She rarely leaves over one thing. She leaves over a pattern nobody fixed.
Overthinking one text, a timeline:
0 min: send a totally normal message.
4 min: reread it. Was the "haha" too much?
20 min: he's online. Not replying. Interesting.
1 hr: screenshot sent to the group chat for full analysis.
3 hrs: drafting "no worries if you're busy!!"
He replies: "sorry, was at the gym." You: completely fine. As if nothing happened.
How to tell attention from real interest:
Attention texts you at midnight. Interest asks about your Friday.
Attention likes your stories. Interest remembers what you said.
Attention disappears when you have needs. Interest asks what you need.
Attention feels exciting. Interest feels calm.
Attention keeps you guessing. Interest makes you sure.
Stop accepting attention when you're asking for interest.
Things I wish I knew before I got ghosted:
Silence is an answer. Not a kind one. But a clear one.
Disappearing says more about their courage than about your worth.
You don't need closure from them. You can write your own ending.
Checking their profile every day keeps the wound open.
The urge to send one last text fades. Usually by day four.
One day you'll notice you stopped waiting. That's the closure.
When he comes back after ghosting, he'll probably say:
"Hey stranger" like you're the one who left.
"I've been going through a lot." Maybe. Ask what changed.
"I think about you all the time." Thinking isn't texting.
"Can we start over?" Only if he's honest about what happened the first time.
A real comeback has an apology, an explanation and a plan. Not a meme at 1am.
Men: what slow replies feel like on the other side:
You think: "I was busy. It's no big deal."
She thinks: "Did I say something wrong?"
You think: "I'll answer properly later."
She thinks: "He answers everyone else."
You don't have to text all day. Just don't leave her in silence with no reason.
One honest line now beats a perfect reply three days later.
Unpopular opinions about texting:
Double texting is fine. Texting a wall for a week is not.
Replying fast doesn't make you desperate. Playing games just makes you tired.
"Let me know" is not a plan.
If your read receipts are on, own your silence.
A 9-minute voice note isn't communication. It's a podcast.
A heart reaction to "I miss you" is not a reply.
Why people come back after ghosting you:
They got bored. You were the familiar, easy option.
They saw you doing well and wanted to know if you'd still answer.
The person they chose instead didn't choose them back.
Sometimes they really did miss you. That still doesn't explain the silence.
You won't always know why they came back.
You only have to decide if you want them back.
Reply times, translated:
Under 5 minutes: phone in hand, you on his mind.
1 hour: a normal person with a life. Breathe.
6 hours: busy, or deciding. The reply will tell you which.
Next day, "sorry!! crazy day": fine once. Watch the pattern.
3 days later, "haha": that's not a reply. That's a receipt.
Speed matters less than this: does the reply sound like he wants to keep talking?
