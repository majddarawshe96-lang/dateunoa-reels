Things nobody tells you about situationships:
You can't lose someone you never really had. But it still hurts like you did.
The "what are we?" talk feels scary because part of you already knows the answer.
Being chosen "sometimes" slowly teaches you to be grateful for crumbs.
Mixed signals are a clear signal. They mean: "I'm not sure about you."
The right person won't make you audition for a title.
Walking away from something undefined isn't failing. It's choosing yourself out loud.
Texts that hurt more than they should:
"lol" in reply to something you were nervous to send.
"sorry, just seeing this" 9 hours later. After watching your story.
"we'll see" when you asked about the weekend.
"you're overthinking it" when you asked one simple question.
Nothing at all. After you finally said "I miss you."
None of these mean you're too much. They mean the reply was too little.
Men, small things she'll remember for years:
You texted "home safe?" without being asked.
You remembered the name of her difficult coworker.
You made the plan. Day. Time. Place. She didn't have to chase it.
You said "I'm not sure yet, but I like you" instead of disappearing.
You were the same man on a Tuesday as you were on Saturday night.
None of it costs money. All of it costs attention.
Signs you're in a situationship, not a relationship:
You know his coffee order but not if he's seeing anyone else.
Plans happen at 10pm. Never at 10am.
You've never met a single one of his friends.
You rehearse your texts so they don't sound like you want more.
You feel closest to him right before he pulls away.
You deserve something you don't have to decode.
What he says vs what he means (situationship edition):
He says: "I don't like labels." He means: "I like having you close without promising anything."
He says: "Let's not rush it." He means: "Let's not define it."
He says: "You're amazing, I just can't right now." He means: "I know what I'd lose. I'm still not choosing."
He says: "I've never felt like this." He means: maybe exactly that. Watch what he does next week.
Words open the door. Consistency is what walks through it.
She didn't "suddenly" lose interest. Here's what actually happened:
She asked to see you. You said "this week's crazy." Three weeks in a row.
She shared something real. You replied with a meme.
She stopped double-texting. You thought that meant everything was fine.
She got quieter. When she finally explained, you called it drama.
By the time she left, she'd been leaving for months.
She rarely leaves over one thing. She leaves over a pattern nobody fixed.
Overthinking one text, a timeline:
0 min: send a totally normal message.
4 min: reread it. Was the "haha" too much?
20 min: he's online. Not replying. Interesting.
1 hr: screenshot sent to the group chat for full analysis.
3 hrs: drafting "no worries if you're busy!!"
He replies: "sorry, was at the gym." You: completely fine. As if nothing happened.
How to tell attention from real interest:
Attention texts you at midnight. Interest asks about your Friday.
Attention likes your stories. Interest remembers what you said.
Attention disappears when you have needs. Interest asks what you need.
Attention feels exciting. Interest feels calm.
Attention keeps you guessing. Interest makes you sure.
Stop accepting attention when you're asking for interest.
Things I wish I knew before I got ghosted:
Silence is an answer. Not a kind one. But a clear one.
Disappearing says more about their courage than about your worth.
You don't need closure from them. You can write your own ending.
Checking their profile every day keeps the wound open.
The urge to send one last text fades. Usually by day four.
One day you'll notice you stopped waiting. That's the closure.
When he comes back after ghosting, he'll probably say:
"Hey stranger" like you're the one who left.
"I've been going through a lot." Maybe. Ask what changed.
"I think about you all the time." Thinking isn't texting.
"Can we start over?" Only if he's honest about what happened the first time.
A real comeback has an apology, an explanation and a plan. Not a meme at 1am.
Men: what slow replies feel like on the other side:
You think: "I was busy. It's no big deal."
She thinks: "Did I say something wrong?"
You think: "I'll answer properly later."
She thinks: "He answers everyone else."
You don't have to text all day. Just don't leave her in silence with no reason.
One honest line now beats a perfect reply three days later.
Unpopular opinions about texting:
Double texting is fine. Texting a wall for a week is not.
Replying fast doesn't make you desperate. Playing games just makes you tired.
"Let me know" is not a plan.
If your read receipts are on, own your silence.
A 9-minute voice note isn't communication. It's a podcast.
A heart reaction to "I miss you" is not a reply.
Why people come back after ghosting you:
They got bored. You were the familiar, easy option.
They saw you doing well and wanted to know if you'd still answer.
The person they chose instead didn't choose them back.
Sometimes they really did miss you. That still doesn't explain the silence.
You won't always know why they came back.
You only have to decide if you want them back.
Reply times, translated:
Under 5 minutes: phone in hand, you on his mind.
1 hour: a normal person with a life. Breathe.
6 hours: busy, or deciding. The reply will tell you which.
Next day, "sorry!! crazy day": fine once. Watch the pattern.
3 days later, "haha": that's not a reply. That's a receipt.
Speed matters less than this: does the reply sound like he wants to keep talking?
How to tell "busy" from "pulling away":
Busy tells you before the hard week starts.
Busy replies short, but the short reply is warm.
Busy picks the conversation back up where you left it.
Busy says "I'll call Sunday." And Sunday, your phone rings.
Pulling away gets vague, then quiet, then "sorry, crazy month."
Busy people still make room for you. They just tell you where you stand.
Texts people send instead of saying "I'm not interested":
"I've been so busy lately." For three weeks. Without one free minute.
"Let's play it by ear." The ear is never available.
"Haha yeah totally" No question back. No plan. Just a full stop.
"I'm not looking for anything serious right now." Believe them the first time.
"You're honestly such a great person." The nicest goodbye there is.
"Not interested" is a full sentence. You can hear it even when they won't say it.
Before you answer someone who ghosted you, ask yourself:
Did they explain why they left, or just say "hey"?
Am I replying because I miss them, or because I miss being wanted?
Would I be okay if they disappeared again next week?
Is the version of me they left behind still the version I am now?
Do I want a conversation, or do I just want the last word?
Whatever you choose, choose it calmly. You're allowed to take your time this time.
The five stages of waiting for a reply:
Stage 1: "He's probably driving."
Stage 2: Reading your own text for the ninth time to check it didn't sound weird.
Stage 3: Asking the group chat for an emergency analysis.
Stage 4: "I don't even care." You care. A lot.
Stage 5: Putting the phone in another room and living your life.
Stage 5 is the one that feels the best. Get there faster next time.
Things I learned from being ghosted:
Silence is an answer. It's just not the one you wanted.
Closure doesn't come from their reply. It comes from your decision.
The people who care don't leave you rereading old messages.
Being ghosted isn't a review of you. It's a preview of them.
And when they come back, you don't owe them the version of you they left behind.
You didn't lose them. You got your evenings back.
Comeback texts, translated:
"Hey stranger" I'm checking if you still answer.
"I saw this and thought of you" I thought of you weeks ago too. I just didn't say it.
"Sorry I've been MIA" I'm hoping you won't ask where I went.
"How have you been?" I want to know if you moved on.
"I owe you an explanation" The only one worth reading.
A comeback text is just a question. You get to choose the answer.
Signs you're finally over the slow replier:
His name popped up and you finished your coffee first.
You stopped checking when he was last online.
Your friends stopped hearing his name at every dinner.
"Hey you" arrived and all you felt was mild curiosity.
You started answering people who answer you.
Not bitter. Not waiting. Just busy with a life that texts back.
The texting timeline of someone who's not that into you:
Week 1: Good-morning texts. Voice notes. "You're so easy to talk to."
Week 2: Replies by lunch. Still a few emojis.
Week 3: "haha" "true" "crazy"
Week 4: Left on delivered. Watches every story.
Week 7: "Hey stranger, how've you been?"
The timeline always tells you. Believe it at week 3, not week 7.
What consistency actually looks like in month three:
He still asks about the thing you were nervous about.
Plans don't need reminders anymore. He just makes them.
Busy weeks get a short "thinking of you" instead of silence.
Your friends stopped asking "so what are you two?" The answer is obvious.
You haven't checked his last-seen in weeks.
Butterflies get you to month one. Consistency is what makes you stay.
Attention vs effort. Learn the difference:
Attention: likes every story you post. Effort: asks how the trip actually went.
Attention: "we should get dinner sometime." Effort: "Thursday at 8? I'll book it."
Attention: texts when he's bored. Effort: texts when he's busy too.
Attention: shows up when it's convenient. Effort: shows up when it isn't.
Attention feels good for a night. Effort is what you can build on.
Small things she notices that cost you nothing:
You put your phone face down when she's talking.
You text "home safe?" after she leaves your place.
You introduce her by name, not "this is my friend."
You remember how she takes her coffee.
You keep the plan even when something better came up.
None of it is big. All of it tells her she's safe with you.
Unpopular opinion: consistency beats chemistry.
Chemistry is the spark on the first date. Consistency is still there on the fortieth.
Chemistry makes you check your phone. Consistency means you don't have to.
Chemistry says "I've never felt this way before." Consistency says "see you Thursday."
Chemistry can fade in one bad week. Consistency shows up for the bad week.
Look for the spark. Then stay for the one who keeps showing up.
Signs you're the only one putting in effort:
You always pick the place. You always pick the time. You always send the first text.
Your good news gets "nice." Their bad day gets your whole evening.
You remember their important dates. They remember yours when Instagram reminds them.
You stopped asking "are we okay?" because the answer is always "yeah why."
Effort should feel like a conversation, not a one-woman show.
What she actually remembers about early dating:
Not the restaurant. That you asked what she was in the mood for.
Not the gift. That you noticed she'd mentioned it once.
Not the long texts. That you replied on your busiest day.
Not the compliments. That you defended her when she wasn't in the room.
She forgets the details. She never forgets how safe you made her feel.

He tells you when he'll be late before you have to ask.
He doesn't disappear when work gets busy. He just texts shorter.
None of it is loud. All of it is steady. That's the point.
Showing off vs. showing up (she always knows which):
Showing off: flowers after a fight. Showing up: no fight needed.
Showing off: a surprise she has to post. Showing up: noticing she was quiet and asking why.
Showing off: big plans for next month. Showing up: being there this Thursday.
Showing off: "I'll change." Showing up: she stops having to ask.
One gets applause. The other gets her trust.

You ask her opinion before you decide, not after.
You text her the boring stuff too. Not only the highlights.
You don't need a reason to call. You just wanted to hear how her day went.
Being liked feels nice. Being chosen feels like peace.
